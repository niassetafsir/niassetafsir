الدرس الثاني — Al-Baqara 2:6–25

"I seek refuge in Allāh from Satan the accursed. By Allāh's Name, The Entirely Merciful, The Especially Merciful."

O Allah, bestow blessings upon our master Muḥammad, the opener of what had been closed and the seal of what had preceded, the helper of truth through truth, the guide to Your straight path, and upon his family, the true measure of his worth and his immense station. May Allah be pleased with the Companions of the Messenger of Allah, ṣ.a.w.s.

Having described the elect among His slaves with those qualities and mentioned their ultimate destination, He followed this with the description of His enemies. He dedicated four verses to the characteristics of the believers, two verses to the disbelievers, and thirteen verses to the hypocrites.

Kufr (disbelief) linguistically means covering and concealing. In the terminology of Islamic law, it signifies the denial of something whose arrival from the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., is necessarily known. Al-kāfir (the disbeliever) appears in the Qurʾān in four categories: (1) the disbeliever as the opposite of the believer, as in "Verily those who disbelieve and hinder from the path of Allah" (Q. 4:167); (2) the disbeliever in the sense of one who denies, as in "whoever rejects it — Allah is Self-Sufficient, above all the worlds" (Q. 3:97), meaning whoever denies the obligation of ḥajj; (3) the disbeliever as the opposite of the grateful, as in "Give thanks to Me and do not disbelieve" (Q. 2:152); and (4) the disbeliever in the sense of one who disavows, as in "then on the Day of Resurrection you will disavow one another and curse one another" (Q. 29:25), meaning you will disavow one another.¹

The varieties of kufr are as follows. The kufr of the knowing denier: one who knows the existence of the True, Blessed and Exalted, and yet denies Him by tongue, as in the kufr of Iblīs and the kufr of the Jews: "and when there came to them that which they recognized, they disbelieved in it" (Q. 2:89). The kufr of absolute negation: denying the existence of the Creator entirely, as with most disbelievers. The kufr of one who knows the truth inwardly but does not profess it by tongue and does not practice the religion, as occurred with Abū Ṭālib, who said in verse: "Verily I know that the religion of Muḥammad / is among the best of all the religions of mankind." He uttered this with his tongue and believed it in his heart, yet he did not enter Islām and thus died a disbeliever. And the kufr of hypocrisy (al-nifāq): professing belief by tongue while denying it in the heart.

Kufr, as we established, is the denial of something whose arrival from the Prophet Muḥammad, ṣ.a.w.s., is necessarily known: the oneness of Allah, Blessed and Exalted; the performance of prayers; fasting Ramaḍān; the obligatory nature of zakāh on wealth; ḥajj; and the Resurrection. Whatever is necessarily known to have come from the Prophet, its denial is kufr. As for what cannot be known to have come from the Prophet except through careful investigation and discernment, one who denies it does not become a disbeliever. The distinguishing marks which the scholars have enumerated as acts of kufr — such as wearing the zunnār (the monk's belt) and the ghiyār — are signs of kufr, not kufr in themselves. Whoever is seen wearing the zunnār is declared with certainty to be no believer, because the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., forbade it; yet it carries no self-gratification the way fornication or wine-drinking do. Those are forbidden by the Law, but they contain pleasure for the lower self, and whoever commits them is not a disbeliever because the self overcame him through desire. But wearing the zunnār carries no pleasure whatsoever: whoever wears it has contemptuously dismissed the Islamic religion, and he is a disbeliever. Anyone who meets Allah, Blessed and Exalted, bearing any form of kufr will dwell forever in the Fire: "Verily Allah does not forgive that anything be associated with Him, and He forgives what is less than that for whomever He wills" (Q. 4:48).

(Verily those who disbelieve) — that is, people such as Abū Jahl, Abū Lahab, and those like them among the disbelievers — (it is equal to them whether you warn them or you do not warn them: they will not believe), because Allah, Blessed and Exalted, knew this of them; so do not aspire to their faith.

The True, Blessed and Exalted, informed His Prophet that these disbelievers would never believe. He did not say "it is equal to you whether you warn them or not," for warning and leaving warning are not equal concerning the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., since he is obligated to convey the message. If he conveys it, whether anyone believes or not, he is rewarded. But as for the disbelievers who will not believe: warning and not warning them are equal to them, for they will never believe. Allah informed His Prophet of this in advance so as to cut off his hope for their faith, so that he might not suffer as Nūḥ suffered. After spending one thousand minus fifty years among his people, Allah revealed to him: "None of your people will believe except those who have already believed" (Q. 11:36). After enduring those tribulations, Allah informed him. But the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., was told in advance, so that he would not long for their faith. And when Nūḥ came to know that his people would not believe, he called down destruction upon them; but the Prophet, upon learning that his people would not believe, did not call down destruction upon them, for he is the Prophet of Mercy: "We did not send you save as a mercy to all the worlds" (Q. 21:107).

Allah, Blessed and Exalted, established in this verse that warning them and not warning them are equal. The disbelievers themselves acknowledged this regarding their prophet Hūd: "It is equal to us whether you admonish us or are not among those who admonish" (Q. 26:136). And in the hereafter, when they are cast into the Fire, they will be told: "Bear it patiently or do not — it is the same for you" (Q. 52:16). Just as Allah declared it equal for them in this world regarding warning, so they are made to know in the Fire that their patience and their impatience are alike. And they themselves say: "Whether we are distressed or patient, it is the same for us: there is no way out for us" (Q. 14:21).

(Allah has set a seal on their hearts), stamped upon them and locked them so that no good enters them, (and on their hearing), so they are unable to benefit from the truth they hear, (and on their eyes is a covering), so they cannot see the truth. (And for them is a mighty punishment): a powerful and eternal chastisement, which is the Fire. "Mighty" (ʿaẓīm) is the opposite of paltry, just as "great" (kabīr) is the opposite of small, and ʿaẓīm surpasses kabīr in degree. This punishment is exceedingly severe, severer than every other punishment. This concerns the disbelievers.

And this was revealed concerning the hypocrites, such as ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy ibn Salūl and his companions, who when they came to the Muslims said to them "We are with you," and when they went to the disbelievers said "We are with you."

(And among the people are those who say: "We believe in Allah and in the Last Day"), that is, the Day of Resurrection, called "the Last Day" because it is the final of all days, (yet they are not believers). The plural form is used in accordance with the sense of "those among the people." (They seek to deceive Allah and those who believe), by displaying outwardly the opposite of what they conceal inwardly of disbelief, so as to ward off from themselves the worldly consequences of kufr. (Yet they deceive none but themselves): because the harm of their deception returns to them. They are exposed in this world through Allah's informing His Prophet of what they concealed, and they will be punished in the hereafter. (And they do not perceive it.)

As for the grammatical note: yukhādiʿūna here is a form of the pattern fāʿala, which normally denotes mutuality between two parties, but here it applies to one party alone, just as one might say in Arabic "I punished the thief" or "I treated the patient," using fāʿala without a partner. The mention of Allah in "they seek to deceive Allah" is an elevation of the Muslims' station, for in truth they are only deceiving the believers; but Allah made the deception of the Muslims equivalent to deceiving Him, Blessed and Exalted.

(In their hearts is a disease), that is, doubt and hypocrisy, which ail their hearts. (So Allah increased their disease) through what He sent down of the Qurʾān, because they rejected it. This disease may be understood as spiritual: the doubt and hypocrisy in their hearts is a moral sickness. Or it may be understood as a genuine physical pain, because the continuous and ever-increasing strength of the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., and the growing might of the Muslims caused them anguish until it became as though a bodily disease. (And for them is a painful punishment), a torment that causes pain, (because they used to lie) — in the reading of Ḥafṣ — or "because they used to disbelieve" in the reading of Warsh.²

(And when it is said to them), these hypocrites, ("Do not spread corruption in the earth"), through disbelief and diverting people from faith, (they say: "We are only reformers"), claiming that what they do is not corruption but rather merely maintaining relations with both sides and serving their own interests. "Innahum hum al-mufsidūn" carries double emphasis. But the triple emphasis with the lām of affirmation is absent, because they do not actively perceive their own corruption; they genuinely believe themselves to be reformers.

A philosopher once posed a question to a scholar: "We see redundancy in Arabic speech — people say 'Zayd is standing,' then 'Indeed Zayd is standing,' then 'Indeed Zayd is certainly standing.' Is this not mere padding?" The Arab scholar replied: "'Zayd is standing' is a straightforward declaration of Zayd's standing. 'Indeed Zayd is standing' is an answer to an implied question. 'Indeed Zayd is certainly standing' is a refutation of someone who denies it." The hypocrites said "We believe" using a verbal form (āmannā), a lighter and less committed expression. Allah replied to them: "They are not believers," using the nominal form with the negation of mā, which is stronger, for it expels them entirely from the totality of believers. Had He said only "they did not believe," that alone would not have removed them from the community of believers.

(And He extends them in their transgression), giving them respite in their exceeding of all bounds through kufr, (wandering blindly). (Those are they who purchased error in exchange for guidance): they took misguidance in place of guidance. (So their trade brought no profit, and they were not guided) in what they did.

Then Allah struck two parables for the hypocrites, for a parable affords the clearest possible illustration: it represents what is absent through what is present, and what is conceptual through what is sensory. (Their likeness), their condition in their hypocrisy, (is as the likeness of one who kindled a fire), one who lit a fire in darkness. (When it illumined all around him), when he could see and had found warmth and protection from what he feared, (Allah took away their light), extinguishing it. He did not say "took away their fire" because the purpose in kindling a fire is the light it produces. (And He left them in darknesses unable to see), confused and confounded about the way, fearful. (Or like a rainstorm from the sky), or their likeness is as those beneath a rain shower, (in which are darknesses): the darkness of night, the darkness of heavy cloud, and the darkness of falling rain. (And thunder), the angel charged with the rain, or its sound. (And lightning), its flash. (They put their fingers into their ears against the thunderbolts, fearing death) from hearing it. Just so, these hypocrites: when the Qurʾān descended containing the mention of kufr, likened to darkness, and threats against it, likened to thunder, and its clear proofs, likened to lightning, they stopped their ears so as not to hear it, lest they be moved to believe in what they heard that they desired, and lest they be held to what they loathed. This is the parable of the hypocrites.³

The hypocrite is a disbeliever at heart; yet seeing the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., bearing a sword, killing those who opposed him and seizing their wealth, he came and pronounced the two testimonies of faith: "There is no god but Allah; Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah," though he was not sincere in his heart. He prayed with the Muslims and gave charity. By this he protected himself and his wealth in life. But death is inevitable; and when he dies, he enters a punishment far more severe than what he had feared in life.

The Qurʾān has now established the descriptions of the believers, the disbelievers, and the hypocrites. The Qurʾān began with the "clear" (muḥkam), Sūrat al-Fātiḥa, a perfectly composed sūra, and followed with Sūrat al-Baqara, the first verse of which is "ambiguous" (mutashābih): "Alif. Lām. Mīm." These letters are the weightiest thing for the Arabs, because they understood nothing of what these letters contain. The Qurʾān, in its miraculous nature, is recognized through its eloquence, its rhetorical excellence, and its knowledge of the unseen. The Arabs understood everything that appeared in the Qurʾān until these letters appeared at the openings of certain sūras, and they could discern not the faintest trace of their meaning. These letters are the hardest thing upon them.

Every Arabic word drawn from the roots of the language carries a meaning that matches the nature of the root. For example, the root k-l-m carries in the Arabic language the meaning of force and intensity. This meaning appears in all its derivations: the kalima (word) is so called because it strikes the hearing and the mind of a person until he grasps its meaning; kalam in another sense is the grievous wound, a severe strike. Every word of the Arabs has a derivation and variations. The kalima appears in the Qurʾān in the sense of ʿĪsā, who is called "a word" because he came into being from the command "kun" (Be!), without the agency of a father. And in the usage of the Arabs, they call a full ode a kalima and a sermon a kalima. The Messenger, ṣ.a.w.s., said: "The truest word the poet ever uttered is the word of Labīd: 'Truly, everything besides Allah is vanishing.'" Yet these disconnected letters: the Arabs found in them no trace of meaning. They are therefore the greatest miracle for the Arabs, surpassing all the other miracles of the Qurʾān.⁴⁵

When the first of the disconnected letters, "Kāf. Hā. Yā. ʿAyn. Ṣād.," was revealed, Jibrīl would normally recite the whole verse to the end and then explain it, and the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., would repeat it as Jibrīl had recited it and explain it as well. But when Jibrīl brought the revelation "Kāf," the Prophet said: "I understand." "Hāʾ" — "I understand." "Yāʾ" — "I understand." "ʿAyn" — "I understand." "Ṣād" — "I understand." The Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., knew their meanings before Jibrīl knew them. Jibrīl thereby came to know that this is a secret concealed even from him, the very one charged with conveying the revelation.⁶

The Sheikh, r.a., describes the believers and adorns them with the finest qualities: taqwā (God-consciousness), faith in the unseen (al-ghayb), the establishment of prayer (iqāmat al-ṣalāh), and spending from what Allah has provided them.

Prayer is mentioned by Allah in the Qurʾān in a number of verses: "Establish the prayer" (aqīmū al-ṣalāh), meaning not merely to perform a prayer but to establish it, to erect it and perfect it and correct it in every aspect so that it contains no deficiency. Allah commanded the performance of prayer in congregation: "and bow with those who bow" (Q. 2:43). And He commanded that it be performed with full inward submission: "those who are humble in their prayers" (Q. 23:2).

The Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., and those who followed him, may Allah make us and you among them, fulfilled all of these commands entirely: they established the prayer, performed it in congregation and in its proper time, and humbled themselves within it. Allah mentioned them and mentioned their final destination: "Those are the inheritors who shall inherit al-Firdaws; therein they shall abide" (Q. 23:10-11).

He also mentioned those who stand in contrast: those who "belied and turned away" (Q. 75:32) and their recompense: "What has brought you into Saqar? They said: 'We were not among those who prayed'" (Q. 74:42-43). And those who came after and neglected prayer: "Then there followed after them a generation who wasted the prayer and followed desires; they shall encounter perdition" (Q. 19:59). Whenever Allah mentions prayer, He joins it with zakāh, because prayer is the greatest act of bodily worship and spending in charity is the greatest act of worship with wealth; and no one's religion is complete unless he worships with both his body and his wealth.

Having described the disbelievers, the hypocrites, and the believers, Allah, Blessed and Exalted, then called all of humanity to His oneness, which is the greatest of all things.

(O Mankind), the people of Mecca and all who surround them from throughout the world, (worship), that is, unify, (your Lord who created you), who brought you into existence when you were nothing, (and those before you), and who created those before you, (so that perhaps you might be God-conscious): laʿalla in the speech of Allah, Blessed and Exalted, carries the force of certainty and confirmation. But from the perspective of the slave: Allah commands him to worship and to unify his Lord, yet the slave must not feel safe but must rather hope and fear. Thus "perhaps you might be God-conscious" requires no reinterpretation; the slave hopes to be among the muttaqīn and fears that he may not attain it.

He then explains the rationale for singling out Allah alone for worship: (He who made the earth a resting place for you), a spread-out expanse upon which to recline, not so hard that nothing can find rest upon it nor so soft that nothing can stand upon it. Allah made it firm yet yielding so that you may settle upon it standing, seated, or lying down. (And the sky a canopy): He who raised this roof above us. (And sent down from the sky water): the rain descends first from the sky, then reaches the clouds, then descends from the clouds to the earth. (By it He brought forth all kinds of fruits as provision for you), which you eat and with which you feed your animals. (So do not set up rivals to Allah), partners in worship, (while you know): do not worship other than Allah, Blessed and Exalted, while you know that He alone is the Creator. If a person knows that Allah is the Creator, he knows that none deserves his worship except Allah.

Among those of the deluded: one who knows that Allah created him yet does not worship Him; one who knows that Allah provides for him yet does not rely upon Him; one who knows this world passes away yet clings to it; one who knows his heirs will be his adversaries yet gathers wealth for them; one who knows death is coming yet has not prepared for it; one who knows with certainty the grave is his dwelling yet has not prepared it; one who knows with certainty the Bridge (al-ṣirāṭ) is his crossing yet has not lightened his load; one who knows with certainty the Fire is the abode of the wicked yet has not fled from it; one who knows with certainty Paradise is the dwelling of the beloved of Allah yet does not seek it; and one who knows with certainty that Iblīs is his enemy yet does not do battle with him.⁷

The categories of shirk (associating partners with Allah) are three: the manifest shirk, that practiced by the polytheists in the worship of idols; the hidden shirk, acting for the sake of reputation and public praise; and the most hidden shirk, perceiving anything other than Allah, Blessed and Exalted.

It is narrated that Allah has charged seven angels with the deeds of the slave. If he performs an act, the angels carry it upward; the angel of the lowest heaven examines it and says: "Return this deed upon its owner — he is a slanderer" or "a backbiter." In each successive heaven, deeds of arrogance, self-admiration, jealousy, ostentation, and love of fame are similarly returned. If a deed passes through all the heavens, Allah, Blessed and Exalted, says: "This person concealed from you what lies within him; but nothing is concealed from Me. This deed was not intended for My face — return it upon its owner, for he intended other than Me." For this reason it is narrated from the Messenger, ṣ.a.w.s.: "People are destroyed save the believers; the believers are destroyed save the scholars; the scholars are destroyed save those who act; those who act are destroyed save the sincere; and the sincere are in a state of peril."⁸

The Qurʾān first challenged with the whole Qurʾān itself: "Say: if all of mankind and the jinn were to gather to produce the like of this Qurʾān, they could not produce the like of it, even if they were to one another supporters" (Q. 17:88). The Arabs, with all their eloquence and rhetorical mastery, did not come forward to compete with the Prophet, ṣ.a.w.s., in producing the like of the Qurʾān. The only one who dared was Musaylama the Arch-Liar, and Allah so disfigured his attempt that his words sounded as though they were not those of an Arab at all. His imitation of Sūrat al-Kawthar was: "We have given you jewels; pray to your Lord and make yourself manifest." What connection is there between jewels and prayer? Jewels are something of the adornments of this world, far removed from "We have surely given you al-Kawthar; so pray to your Lord and sacrifice."⁹

The Americans once commissioned a man learned in Arabic to compose something like Sūrat al-Fātiḥa. He said: "In the name of the Lord of all existence," abandoning the name Allāh, which is indispensable in speaking of Allah, Blessed and Exalted, and saying instead "Lord of all existence" (rabb al-akwān), abandoning "all the worlds" (al-ʿālamīn) for a modern coinage not found in classical Arabic. He then said "Yours is the worship and in You I seek help," intending to condense "You alone we worship and from You alone we seek help" (iyyāka naʿbudu wa-iyyāka nastaʿīn) into four words; but he turned al-mustaʿān into a verbal noun, distorting the grammar entirely. Anyone who hears these words knows immediately that they are not the speech of a native Arab, that the speaker has never mastered classical Arabic.

من زينة الدنيا، هذا بعيد من "إِنَّا أَعْطَيْنَٰكَ الْكَوْتَرَ بَصَلٍ لِرَبِّكَ وَانْحَرِ . واكزى الأمريكيون واحدا يعلم العربية أن يصنع مثل فاتحة الكتاب، فتكلم بألفاظ كل من سمعها لا يظن أن المتكلم بهذا عربي يقول: باسم رب الأكوان بدلا من "إِلْحَمْدُ لِلهِ رَبِّ ٱلْعَلَمِينَ"، باسم رب الأكوان الرحمن الرحيم لك العبادة وبك المستعان، ترك اللّٰه الذي هو لا يُستغنَى عنه في ذكر اللّٰه تبارك وتعالى قال: باسم رب الأكوان، وترك العالمين وقال الأكوان، الأكوان جمع كون وليس في اللغة العربية القديمة الفصحى جمع الأكوان، إنما هي كلمة محدثة، قال لك العبادة بدلا من إياك نعبد وهو يقول إنه يختصر إياك نعبد وإياك نستعين أربع كلمات، ولك العبادة وبك المستعان: المستعان جعله مصدرا، تعلمون أن هذا ممسوخ الكلام بعيد من الفاتحة. تفسير الجلالين
Tafsīr al-Jalālayn · English trans. Feras Hamza
[2:6] As for the disbelievers, the likes of Abū Jahl, Abū Lahab and such; alike it is for them whether you have warned them or have not warned them, they do not believe, as God knows very well, so do not hope that they will believe (read a-andhartahum pronouncing both hamzas, or by not pronouncing the second, making it an alif instead, and inserting an alif between the one not pronounced and the other one, or leaving [this insertion]; al-indhār [‘warning’] is to give knowledge of something, and simultaneously instil an element of fear). [2:7] God has set a seal on their hearts, impressing on them and making certain that no good enters them; and on their hearing, [in which He has] deposited something so that they cannot profit from the truth they hear; and on their eyes is a covering, that is, a veil so that they do not see the truth; and for them there will be a mighty chastisement, that is, intense and everlasting. [2:8] The following was revealed concerning the hypocrites: and some people there are who say, ‘We believe in God and the Last Day’, that is, in the Day of Resurrection because it is the very last day; but they are not believers (the [plural] import of man [in man yaqūl, ‘who says’] is taken into account here, as expressed by a pronoun [hum] that expresses this [plural] meaning). 3 [2:9] They would deceive God and the believers, by manifesting the opposite of the unbelief they hide, so that they can avoid His rulings in this world; and only themselves they deceive (yukhādi‘ūn), for the evil consequences of their deception will rebound upon them, as they are disgraced in this world when God makes known to His Prophet what they are hiding, and they will be punished in the Hereafter; and they are not aware, and they do not know that they are actually deceiving themselves (mukhāda‘a [although a third verbal form, from khāda‘a] actually denotes a one-way action, such as [when one says] ‘āqabtu al-lissa, ‘I punished the thief’ [using the third verbal form ‘āqaba]; the mention of ‘God’ in [this statement] is for [rhetorical] effect; a variant reading [for wa-mā yukhādi‘ūna] has wa-mā yakhda‘ūna). [2:10] In their hearts is a sickness: doubt and hypocrisy, which ails their hearts, debilitating them; and God has increased their sickness with what He has revealed in the Qur’ān, since they disbelieve it; and there awaits them a painful chastisement because they used to lie (read yukadhdhibūn to imply [that they used to call] the Prophet of God [a liar], or yakdhibūn to imply their [mendacity when] saying ‘we believe’). [2:11] When it is said to them, that is, these latter, ‘Do not spread corruption in the land’, through unbelief and hindering [people from] faith, They say, ‘We are only putting things right’, that is, ‘we are not engaging in corruption’. God, exalted be He, refutes them, saying: [2:12] Truly (a-lā, ‘truly’, is for alerting), intended emphatically, they are the agents of corruption, but they perceive, this, not. [2:13] When it is said to them, ‘Believe as the people believe’, that is, as the Companions of the Prophet (s), They say, ‘Shall we believe as fools believe?’, that is, as the ignorant do? No we do not follow their way. The exalted One refutes them, saying: Truly, they are the foolish ones, but they know, this, not. [2:14] When they meet (laqū is actually laquyū, but the damma has been omitted, being too cumbersome for pronunciation; likewise the yā’ [is omitted], because it is unvocalised and is followed by a wāw); those who believe, they say, ‘We believe’; but when they go apart, away from them and return, to their devils, their leaders, they say, ‘We are with you, in religion; we were only mocking, them [the believers] by feigning belief. [2:15] God [Himself] mocks them, requiting them for their mockery, leaving them, that is, giving them respite, in their insolence, that is, in their transgressing the limits of unbelief; bewildered, wavering, in perplexity (ya‘mahūn is a circumstantial qualifier). [2:16] Those are they who have bought error for guidance, that is, they have exchanged the latter for the former; so their commerce has not profited them, that is to say, they have gained nothing from it, indeed, they have lost, because their destination is the Fire, made everlasting for them; nor are they guided, in what they did. [2:17] 4 Their likeness, the way they are in their hypocrisy, is as the likeness of one who kindled, that is, [one who] lit a fire in darkness, and when it illumined all about him, so that he is able to see, and to feel warm and secure from those he feared, God took away their light, extinguishing it (the plural pronoun [in nūrihim] takes into account the [plural] import of alladhī); and left them in darkness, unable to see, what is around them, confused as to the way, in fear; likewise are those who have found [temporary] security by professing faith, but who will meet with terror and punishment upon death; these [last] are: [2:18] deaf, to the truth, so that they cannot hear it and accept it; dumb, mute as regards goodness, unable to speak of it; and, blind, to the path of guidance, so that they cannot perceive it; they shall not return, from error. [2:19] Or, the likeness of them is as a cloudburst, that is, [the likeness of them is] as people are during rain (kasayyib: the term is originally sayyūb, from [the verb] sāba, yasūbu, meaning ‘it came down’); out of the heaven, out of the clouds, in which clouds is darkness, layer upon layer, and thunder, the angel in charge of them [sc. the clouds]; it is also said that this [thunder] is actually the sound of his voice; and lightning, the flash caused by his voice which he uses to drive them — they, the people under the rain, put their fingers, that is, their fingertips, in their ears against, because of, the thunderclaps, the violent sound of thunder, in order not to hear it, cautious of, fearful of, death, if they were to hear it. Similar is the case with these: when the Qur’ān is revealed, in which there is mention of the unbelief that is like darkness, the threat of punishment that is like the sound of thunder, and the clear arguments that are like the clear lightning, they shut their ears in order not to hear it and thereby incline towards [true] faith and abandon their religion, which for them would be death; and God encompasses the disbelievers in both knowledge and power, so they cannot escape Him. [2:20] The lightning well-nigh, almost, snatches away their sight, that is, takes it away swiftly; whensoever it gives them light, they walk in it, in its light; and when the darkness is over them, they stop, that is, they stand still: a simile of the perturbation that the Qur’ānic arguments cause in their hearts, and of their acknowledging the truths of what they love to hear and recoiling from what they detest; had God willed, He would have taken away their hearing and their sight, that is, the exterior faculty, in the same way that He took away their inner one; Truly, God has power over all things, [that] He wills, as for example, His taking away of the above-mentioned. [2:21] O people, of Mecca, worship, profess the oneness of, your Lord Who created you, made you when you were nothing, and created those that were before you; so that you may be fearful, of His punishment by worshipping Him (la‘alla, ‘so that’, is essentially an optative, but when spoken by God it denotes an affirmative), [2:22] He Who assigned to you, created [for you], the earth for a couch, like a carpet that is laid out, neither extremely hard, nor extremely soft so as to make it impossible to stand firm upon it; and heaven for an edifice, like a roof; and sent down from the heaven water, wherewith He brought forth, all types of, fruits for your provision; so set not up compeers to God, that is partners in worship, while you know that He is the Creator, that you create not and that only One that creates can be God. [2:23] 5 And if you are in doubt, in uncertainty, concerning what We have revealed to Our servant, Muhammad (s), of the Qur’ān, that it is from God, then bring a sūra like it, that is also revealed (min mithlihi: min is explicative, that is, a sūra like it in its eloquence, fine arrangement and its bestowal of knowledge of the Unseen; a sūra is a passage with a beginning and end made up of a minimum of three verses); and call your witnesses, those other gods that you worship, besides God, that is, other than Him, so that it can be seen, if you are truthful, in [your claim] that Muhammad (s) speaks it from himself. So do this, for you are also fluent speakers of Arabic like him. When they could not do this, God said: [2:24] And if you do not, do what was mentioned because you are incapable, and you will not (a parenthetical statement), that is, never [will you be able to], because of its inimitability, then fear, through belief in God and [belief] that this is not the words of a human, the Fire, whose fuel is men, disbelievers, and stones, like their very idols, indicating that its heat is extreme, since it burns with the [stones] mentioned, unlike the fires of this world that burn with wood and similar materials; prepared, and made ready, for disbelievers, so that they are punished in it (this [phrase, u‘iddat li’l-kāfirīna, ‘prepared for disbelievers’] is either a new sentence or a sustained circumstantial qualifier). [2:25] And give good tidings to, inform, those who believe, who have faith in God, and perform righteous deeds, such as the obligatory and supererogatory [rituals], that theirs shall be Gardens, of trees, and habitations, underneath which, that is, underneath these trees and palaces, rivers run (tajrī min tahtihā’l-anhāru), that is, there are waters in it (al-nahr is the place in which water flows [and is so called] because the water carves [yanhar] its way through it; the reference to it as ‘running’ is figurative); whensoever they are provided with fruits therefrom, that is, whenever they are given to eat from these gardens, they shall say, ‘This is what, that is, the like of what we were provided with before’, that is, before this, in Paradise, since its fruits are similar (and this is evidenced by [the following statement]): they shall be given it, the provision, in perfect semblance, that is, resembling one another in colour, but different in taste; and there for them shall be spouses, of houris and others, purified, from menstruation and impurities; therein they shall abide: dwelling therein forever, neither perishing nor departing therefrom. And when the Jews said, ‘Why does God strike a similitude about flies, where He says, And if a fly should rob them of anything [Q. 22:73] and about a spider, where He says, As the likeness of the spider [Q. 29:41]: what does God want with these vile creatures?
